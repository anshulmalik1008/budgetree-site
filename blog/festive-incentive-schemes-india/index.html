What is a festive or incentive scheme?

A scheme is a limited-period programme where people earn rewards for hitting a goal. The goal could be a sales target, a purchase volume, a customer referral, or just taking part in a festive campaign. The reward is what turns a target into something people actually want to chase.

Companies run schemes for several groups of people:

  • Dealers and distributors: rewards for bigger purchase volumes during the peak season.

  • Retailers and channel partners: rewards for repeat orders, stock lifting and loyalty.

  • Sales teams: contests with milestones and a leaderboard.

  • Employees: festive or milestone rewards around Diwali, New Year or work anniversaries.

  • Customers: thank-you rewards for buying, referring or staying loyal.

Most schemes follow one of four structures. In a slab-based scheme, a higher target earns a bigger reward. In a target-based scheme, one goal earns one reward. A contest or leaderboard rewards the top performers only. An instant-reward scheme gives something right after a purchase. Plenty of companies mix two or more of these.

Whatever the structure, every scheme makes the same promise. Participants are told what they can win, and the company has to deliver it. That's where most schemes are won or lost.

Why schemes fall apart when rewards come from too many vendors?

Hardly any company starts with a single reward partner. Vendors get added over time: one for gift cards, one for electronics, one for appliances, and a local dealer for vehicles. On paper that looks like a lot of choice. Once the scheme is live, it turns into five familiar headaches.

  1. Too many people to chase. Each vendor has its own contact, terms and deadlines, so your sales operations or marketing team ends up managing a mini project for every one of them.

  2. A patchy reward list. Schemes work better when people can pick what they really want. If no single vendor covers everything, you either limit the choices or spend weeks stitching a list together.

  3. Uneven delivery. One vendor delivers in a week and another takes a month. Winners talk to each other, and the slowest delivery becomes the story everyone remembers.

  4. Messy tracking. Who has claimed what? Which rewards are still pending? With separate vendors, the answers sit in separate spreadsheets.

  5. Too much paperwork. Several purchase orders, invoices and reconciliations mean more work for your finance team and more chances for mistakes.

There's a quieter cost too. A late reward hurts trust in the scheme itself, and dealers and partners remember that when you announce the next one.

The answer isn't to work harder. It's to cut down the number of vendors you depend on.

How to design scheme slabs that keep people chasing?

The best schemes use a reward ladder. Each step feels reachable, and the top step feels worth the effort. Here's a simple example for a festive dealer scheme. The percentages are only for illustration, so set yours based on your own numbers.

  1. Slab 1, the quick win: reach 100% of the base target and earn a gift card. It's easy enough that most dealers get moving.

  2. Slab 2, the everyday reward: reach 125% and earn a home appliance, decor item or tech product.

  3. Slab 3, the aspirational reward: reach 150% and earn a smartphone or laptop.

  4. Slab 4, the grand prize: the top performer in each region wins a two-wheeler or four-wheeler.

A few things make a ladder work. Keep the first slab within reach of most participants. Make each step clearly better than the one before it. Show the grand prize from the first day. And where you can, let winners choose from a good range of rewards.

Since one partner covers every slab, you can plan the whole ladder in a single conversation instead of negotiating with four different vendors.

Scheme ideas and reward picks for every group

A scheme works best when it suits the people in it. A dealer chasing a festive target thinks very differently from a new joiner celebrating a first Diwali with your company. Here's a starting point for each group.

  1. Dealers and distributors: the aim is festive-season sales. Slab-based schemes with appliances, electronics and two-wheelers or four-wheelers as grand prizes tend to work best.

  2. Retailers and channel partners: the aim is repeat orders and loyalty. Instant gift cards, electronics and home appliances are good picks.

  3. Sales team: the aim is to push targets. Use milestone rewards, with gift cards at the start, mobiles and laptops in the middle, and a vehicle for the top performers.

  4. Employees: the aim is appreciation and goodwill. Gift cards, home decor, daily tech products and small appliances usually go down well.

  5. Customers and referrers: the aim is to say thank you and get more referrals. Instant gift cards and premium tech products suit this group.

A simple rule of thumb: use gift cards for flexibility, products for excitement, and big-ticket rewards for the moments that deserve a proper celebration.

How to plan and run a festive scheme in 7 steps

  1. Decide what the scheme is for. Is it higher festive sales, more repeat orders, stronger dealer loyalty or employee appreciation? A clear goal makes every other decision easier.

  2. Know who's taking part. Split them into dealers, retailers, the sales team, employees or customers. What motivates each group, and what they consider a good reward, is different.

  3. Set targets and a budget. Base targets on past numbers and realistic growth. Include the cost of rewards, delivery and communication, and keep a small buffer.

  4. Design the slabs and rewards. Build the reward ladder with a mix of gift cards, products and one or two grand prizes. Keep the lower steps achievable and the top step aspirational.

  5. Write simple rules. Cover who is eligible, the scheme period, how slabs are calculated and how rewards are claimed. Unclear rules are the quickest way to end up with disputes.

  6. Launch with clear communication. Share the slabs, rewards and dates in a simple, visual format, and put the grand prize front and Centre.

  7. Track, deliver and follow up. Watch progress, send reminders at milestones, confirm deliveries and ask for feedback. A scheme only works if winners get their rewards on time and are happy with them.

A single reward partner makes steps 4 to 7 much easier, because the catalogue, delivery and tracking are all handled by one team.

A simple planning calendar

  • 6 to 8 weeks before launch: fix the goal, participants, targets and budget, and shortlist reward categories.

  • 4 to 6 weeks before: finalize the slabs and rules, and confirm your reward partner and catalogue.

  • 2 to 4 weeks before: announce the scheme with clear communication and visuals.

  • During the scheme: track progress, send milestone reminders and celebrate early achievers.

  • After it closes: verify results, confirm winners, arrange delivery, collect feedback and note what to change next time.

Five common scheme mistakes

  • Starting too late. Leave enough time for design, communication and delivery.

  • Slabs that are too hard or too easy. If the first slab feels out of reach, people give up. If it's too easy, the scheme adds nothing. Test your targets against past data.

  • Unclear rules. If people can't tell how to win, they lose interest. Keep the rules short.

  • No grand prize to look forward to. A big-ticket reward creates talk and energy, so include at least one.

  • Too many suppliers. Every extra vendor adds delay and risk, so consolidate where you can.

How to choose a reward partner for your scheme?

Before you commit to a partner for your next scheme, put these eight questions to them. A good one will answer all of them without fuss.

  • Range: can they cover gift cards, products and big-ticket rewards for every slab, or will you still need other vendors?

  • Brand variety: do they offer brands your participants will recognize and want?

  • Scale: can they handle a 50-person contest as easily as a scheme with 5,000 dealers?

  • Delivery: how do they manage timelines during the festive rush?

  • Tracking: will you be able to see who has claimed, who is pending and what has been delivered?

  • Support: is there one person accountable for your scheme, or will you be passed between departments?

  • Pricing: are quotes clear, with no surprise delivery or handling charges?

  • Paperwork: do they give you the invoices and records your finance team needs?

If the answer to the first question is no, you're back to the multi-vendor problem this guide started with.

Frequently asked questions about festive and incentive schemes

What is a festive or incentive scheme?

It's a limited-period programme where people earn rewards for reaching a goal, such as a sales target, a purchase volume or a referral count. Companies run them for dealers, distributors, retailers, sales teams, employees and customers.

What types of schemes can a company run?

The common ones are slab-based schemes (a higher target earns a bigger reward), target-based schemes (one goal, one reward), contests or leaderboards (only the top performers win) and instant-reward schemes (a reward right after a purchase). Many companies combine two or more.

Why use one reward partner instead of several vendors?

With one partner you have one contact, one process and one place to track everything. With several vendors you deal with different timelines, payments and follow-ups, and a delay from any one of them can hold up the whole scheme.

What rewards can I include in a scheme through Budgetree?

You can build your scheme around 350+ brand gift cards, electronics, home appliances, home decor, daily tech products, mobile phones, laptops, two-wheelers and four-wheelers. That covers everything from entry-level rewards to grand prizes.

Should scheme rewards be gift cards or physical products?

It depends on the audience. Gift cards are flexible and suit large groups with different tastes. Physical products feel more exciting and personal. Many good schemes use both, with cards in the lower slabs and products in the higher ones.

Can I offer a two-wheeler or four-wheeler as a scheme reward?

Yes. Vehicles make excellent grand prizes because they give participants something big to aim for. Most companies keep them for the highest slab or for the top performer in each region.

How early should I start planning a festive scheme?

Six to eight weeks before the festival or launch date is a comfortable window. That gives you time to design the slabs, announce the scheme and arrange deliveries without a last-minute rush.

How do I set the right slabs and targets?

Start from past performance and realistic growth. Keep the first slab within reach of most participants, make every step clearly better than the last, and save the grand prize for a real stretch. It helps to look at last year's results before finalizing the numbers.

Do I need to think about tax when running a scheme?

Possibly. The tax treatment of rewards, gifts and vouchers can vary depending on who receives them, their value and how they are given. Please check the current rules with your chartered accountant or tax advisor before you finalize the scheme.
